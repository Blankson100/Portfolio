Clostra

Introduction

Sending money online, whether on Jumia or to someone selling phones at Circle, has always been a gamble. You pay, you wait, and sometimes you never hear back. I experienced it, watched it happen to others, and decided to build a solution.

Clostra is an escrow-based platform that holds funds securely until both parties meet agreed conditions, so payments only release when a deal is done. It works across all kinds of transactions including physical goods, digital products, and services.

An integrated AI assistant guides users through each step, paired with real-time tracking and a transparent transaction history. At its core, Clostra is about one thing: making transactions safer where trust isn't guaranteed.

The Problem

There's no guarantee that money sent online will be honored. A buyer pays upfront and hopes the seller delivers. A seller ships first and hopes the buyer pays. Either way, someone is trusting a stranger with no real recourse if it goes wrong.

Buyers get scammed, sellers go unpaid, and both sides end up relying on screenshots and group chats to settle disputes after the fact, when it's already too late to do anything about it.

What was missing was a neutral third party that holds the money until both sides confirm the deal actually happened, built for how people already buy and sell online.

Design Process

Research started by mapping how people already send and ask for money online: bank transfers, mobile money, DMs confirming "I've sent it." Every one of those flows ends the same way, with no proof until something goes wrong.

The escrow flow went through several versions. Early drafts asked both parties to manually confirm every step, which felt like extra paperwork on top of a deal that should feel simple. The final version collapsed confirmation into one tap, with the AI assistant prompting each side only when it actually needed something from them.

Transaction history and release status needed to be visible at a glance, since the entire point of the product is trust. Status labels, funds held, released, disputed, are shown prominently rather than buried in a details screen.
