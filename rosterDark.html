Roster

Introduction

Roster is an invite-only network for verified professionals to share work, post job opportunities, and connect with people they can actually trust. There are no public profiles and no algorithmic feed, just a private community built one invite at a time.

The idea came from how most professional networks work today: public by default, open to anyone, and ranked by an algorithm with no sense of who actually deserves to be seen. Roster flips that, access comes through an invite, and everything in the feed is filtered by the people already in your network.

It's built for people who'd rather share an opportunity with five people they trust than broadcast it to five thousand strangers.

The Problem

Most job and networking platforms optimize for reach: public profiles, open connection requests, and a feed an algorithm decides to surface. That works for visibility, but it also means spam requests, noise, and real opportunities buried under promoted ones.

For professionals who get their best opportunities through word of mouth, those platforms don't reflect how hiring and referrals actually happen. There was no sense of who had actually vetted the person on the other end of a connection.

What was missing was a network where membership itself was the signal of trust, no profile optimization needed.

Design Process

Research started by looking at why people stop trusting open professional networks, mainly cold outreach and feeds full of promoted content disconnected from their actual circle.

The invite system went through several iterations. An early version let any member invite unlimited people, which diluted trust almost immediately. The final version caps invites per member and ties each new member's visibility to whoever invited them.

The feed itself changed shape too. Early versions mimicked a typical social feed, ranked and algorithmic. Testing showed people wanted a simple chronological list filtered only by their actual network, so that's what shipped.

Removing public profiles entirely was the biggest design risk, since most people expect to browse a directory. The answer was to make connections and invites the only way in, so trust was built into the core interaction instead of a profile page.
