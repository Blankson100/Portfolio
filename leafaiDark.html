Crop Care

Introduction

Crop Care is a plant-disease app for growers. Point your phone at a sick leaf, and it names the likely disease, explains it in plain language, shows how sure it is, and suggests what to do next. Every scan is saved, so over time the app becomes a record of what went wrong and what worked. It's built for a farmer on an open plot and for someone with a few tomato plants at home. The idea came from a school project led by students in another department. I joined as the product designer and took it to a working Figma prototype in two months.

The Problem

Pests and diseases take between a fifth and a third of major staple crops worldwide. In Ghana, one extension officer may serve 700 to 1,500 farmers, and only about 1 in 6 farmers in the North owns a smartphone. In casual chats with farmers near Korle Bu in Accra, none kept a record of crop problems. What they knew lived in memory, passed down from fathers and uncles. So growers face two problems. Right now: what is wrong, and what do I do? Over time: has this happened before, and what worked? Most plant apps answer the first. Crop Care is built for both.

The App

See how it works

Scan a leaf, and the result leads with the disease name, then four plain sections: Symptoms, Cause, Prevention and Treatment. A small coin flips to show how sure the app is: "96%, Very likely". When it isn't sure, it turns amber, says "Not sure" and asks for more leaves. A voice button reads the result aloud and takes questions back. Every scan saves to History automatically, building the crop-health record growers never kept.

Design Process

1.Listening first

  • Casual chats with farmers near Korle Bu, then published research.

Nobody kept records. So remembering became half the app.

2.Camera screen

  • v1no guidance
  • v2written instructions
  • v3two short prompts

Long instructions don't get read.

3.Result screen

  • Before:disease stated as fact.
  • After:confidence shown before you act, and an honest "not sure".

4.The coin

  • v1flipped non-stop
  • v2flips once, then rests on the score

Motion to catch the eye. Stillness to read.

5.Words plus a number

  • "Likely" alone
  • "96%, Very likely"

People read 'likely' very differently.

6.A logbook that fills itself

  • Every scan saves to History automatically.

Records die when they feel like work.

7.Look and feel

  • Greens from healthy leaves.
  • White backgrounds that stay readable outdoors.
