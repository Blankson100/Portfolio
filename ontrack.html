OnTrack

Introduction

OnTrack helps people with diabetes manage their diet and blood sugar day to day. It records glucose readings and turns them into meal recommendations tailored to that person's own numbers, not generic diet advice.

The goal was to make a condition that normally means constant manual tracking feel manageable: log a reading, see what it means, get a suggestion for the next meal, and move on.

It's built to be simple enough to use several times a day without it feeling like a chore, since that's the only way daily tracking actually sticks.

The Problem

Managing diabetes well means tracking blood sugar consistently and connecting it to what you eat, but most people are stuck doing that manually, a paper log or a plain numbers app with no context for what a reading actually means for their next meal.

Without that context, readings pile up but don't change behavior. People see a high number, feel bad about it, and move on without a clear idea of what to adjust.

What was missing was a tool that closes the loop: record the reading, then immediately suggest what to eat next based on it.

Design Process

Research started with how people with diabetes currently log readings, mostly paper logbooks or generic health apps that track the number but never connect it to a next action.

The logging flow went through several versions. The first asked for too much per entry, meal details, time, mood, and testers stopped logging consistently within days. The final version cut it down to the reading itself plus one tap for context, everything else became optional.

The meal recommendation screen changed the most. Early versions gave generic diet advice regardless of the reading. The final version ties the suggestion directly to that entry, so a high reading and a normal reading get visibly different guidance.

Visually, the app leans on a simple progress framing, "every entry is a point on the board", so logging feels like forward motion rather than a chore.
